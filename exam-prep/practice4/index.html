Modules 8 and 9

Module 8: Equity Recognition and Owner Financing

1. Stock Features and Components
Key idea: Equity is divided into Contributed Capital (Common Stock, Preferred Stock, APIC) and Earned Capital (Retained Earnings, AOCI).

Important points:

  • Par Value: An arbitrary legal minimum value assigned to a share. Not related to market value.
  • Authorized vs Issued vs Outstanding: Authorized is the maximum legally permitted. Issued is total given out. Outstanding is Issued minus Treasury Stock.
  • Preferred Stock: Carries a fixed dividend preference over common stock and priority in liquidation, but generally lacks voting rights.
2. Stock Issuances and Repurchases
Key idea: Issuing stock increases assets and equity. Repurchasing stock (Treasury Stock) decreases assets and equity.
  • When stock is issued above par, the excess is recorded as Additional Paid-In Capital (APIC).
  • Treasury Stock: Recorded at cost. It is a contra-equity account (reduces total equity). Companies buy back stock to signal undervaluation, distribute excess cash, or offset dilution from employee stock options.
3. Dividends and Splits
  • Cash Dividends: On the declaration date, retained earnings decreases and dividends payable increases. On the payment date, cash decreases and the liability is removed.
  • Stock Splits: No journal entry required. They proportionally decrease the par value and increase the number of shares. Total equity remains identical.
  • Stock Dividends: Capitalize retained earnings into contributed capital. No change to total equity.
4. Comprehensive Income (AOCI)
Key idea: Comprehensive Income = Net Income + Other Comprehensive Income (OCI).

AOCI includes specific unrealized gains/losses that bypass the income statement, such as foreign currency translation adjustments and unrealized changes in Available-for-Sale securities.

Module 9: Intercorporate Investments

1. Passive Investments (Less than 20% Ownership)
Key idea: Reporting depends heavily on management's intent for the marketable security.
  • Trading Securities: Held for short-term profit. Reported at Fair Value. Unrealized gains/losses flow directly through Net Income.
  • Available-for-Sale (AFS): Available-for-sale debt securities are reported at fair value, with unrealized gains and losses generally reported in OCI/AOCI until realized.
  • Held-to-Maturity (HTM): Debt only. Reported at Amortized Cost. Fair value fluctuations are generally ignored.
2. Equity Method Investments (20% to 50% Ownership)
Key idea: Used when the investor exerts "significant influence" over the investee.

Accounting Mechanics:

  • The investment is initially recorded at historical cost.
  • Net Income: Investor increases the investment account by their percentage share of the investee's net income. This is also reported as income on the investor's income statement.
  • Dividends: When the investee pays a dividend, the investor treats it as a return of capital. It reduces the investment account balance and increases cash. It is NOT recorded as dividend revenue.
