Master Study Guide

Modules 1-3: Analysis Framework

1. The Financial Environment & Profitability
Key idea: Financial reporting connects operations with capital markets via SEC filings.
  • ROA (Return on Assets): Net Income / Avg Assets. Analyzes asset efficiency.
  • ROE (Return on Equity): Net Income / Avg Equity. Measures shareholder returns.
  • DuPont Analysis: ROE = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage.
2. Advanced DuPont (Operating vs Nonoperating)
  • NOPAT: Net Operating Profit After Tax. Measures core operating profit stripped of financing choices. Can be computed as (Net Income + Net Nonoperating Expense after tax) or (NOPBT - Tax on Operating Profit).
  • NNE: Net Nonoperating Expense. Primarily interest expense minus interest income, adjusted for the tax shield.
  • RNOA: Return on Net Operating Assets = NOPAT / Average NOA. Isolates core business return from financing leverage.

Modules 4-5: Credit & Revenue

1. Credit Risk and Covenants
Key idea: Creditors assess liquidity (short-term) and solvency (long-term) to prevent default.
  • Coverage Ratios: Times Interest Earned (EBIT / Int Exp) ensures operations cover debt costs.
  • Z-Score: A predictive model weighing profitability, working capital, and leverage to flag bankruptcy risk. Used by agencies as a quantitative baseline for credit ratings.
2. Revenue Recognition (ASC 606)

The 5 Steps: Identify contract, identify obligations, determine price, allocate price, recognize when satisfied. Deferred Revenue is created when cash is received before the obligation is fulfilled.

Modules 6-7: Assets & Debt

1. Inventory Costing
Key idea: FIFO vs LIFO affects reported value during inflation.
  • During inflation, LIFO = lower net income (tax savings), lower ending inventory.
  • LIFO Reserve: The difference between FIFO and LIFO ending inventory balances. FIFO Inventory = LIFO Inventory + LIFO Reserve.
2. Long-Lived Assets & Intangibles
Key idea: Tangible assets are depreciated, while finite-lived intangibles are amortized to match costs with revenues.
  • Depreciation: Allocates the cost of property, plant, and equipment over its useful life. It is a non-cash expense.
  • Goodwill: Created only during acquisitions (Purchase Price > Fair Value of Net Assets). It is never amortized, but is tested annually for impairment.
3. Bond Pricing and Amortization

Bonds are priced at the Present Value of future cash flows. If the stated coupon rate is lower than the market rate, it sells at a Discount. The Effective Interest Method is used to calculate interest expense, which amortizes the carrying value to par at maturity.
