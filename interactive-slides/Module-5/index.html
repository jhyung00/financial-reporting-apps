Revenue Recognition Principle

Revenue isn't just "when cash is received." GAAP dictates two strict criteria:

1. Earned

(Click to flip)

Earned

Goods or services have been successfully provided/delivered to the customer.

2. Realized

(Click to flip)

Realized

Payment has been received, or a promise of payment (A/R) is reasonably assured.

Drill: The Sales Cycle (1/3)

Scenario: The Order

Nordstrom orders 500 pairs of jeans from Guess for $32 each for future delivery. Terms: Net 30.

Revenue for Guess?

$0

Revenue has not been earned because the jeans have not been delivered.

Drill: The Sales Cycle (2/3)

Scenario: Production

Guess completes the manufacturing of the 500 pairs of jeans in their factory.

Revenue for Guess?

$0

Production is complete, but delivery has not occurred. Control has not transferred.

Drill: The Sales Cycle (3/3)

Scenario: Delivery

Guess puts the jeans on a truck and delivers the order to Nordstrom.

Revenue for Guess?

$16,000

$32 * 500 = $16,000

Revenue is now Earned (delivered) and Realized (promise of payment via A/R).

ASC 606: The 5-Step Model

The modern, comprehensive framework for Revenue Recognition across all industries.

  1. 1. Identify the Contract
  2. 2. Identify Performance Obligations
  3. 3. Determine Transaction Price
  4. 4. Allocate Transaction Price
  5. 5. Recognize Revenue

Breaking Down the Steps

Step 1: The Contract

A contract doesn't have to be written. A verbal order at a fast-food counter is a valid contract. It simply requires commercial substance, identified rights, and probable collection.

Step 2: Performance Obligations (POs)

Identify distinct goods or services promised. For example, buying a McDonald's Extra Value Meal contains 3 distinct obligations: a burger, fries, and a drink.

Step 3: Transaction Price

The total consideration expected. Must account for variable considerations like discounts, rebates, and estimated returns.

Step 4: Allocate Transaction Price

If a contract has multiple obligations, how do we split the single price? We use the Relative Standalone Selling Price (SSP).

Example: Software Bundle

Bundle Price: $100

Standalone Prices if sold separately:

- Software = $80

- Support = $40

Total SSP = $120

The Allocation Math

Software: (80/120) × $100 = $66.67

Support: (40/120) × $100 = $33.33

Total Allocated = $100.00

Step 5: Recognize Revenue

Revenue is booked when the obligation is satisfied. This happens either Over Time or at a Point in Time.

Over Time

Click to see criteria

Continuous Transfer

1. Customer consumes benefit as it's performed (cleaning service).

2. Customer controls asset as created (building on their land).

3. No alternative use for asset + right to payment.

Point in Time

Click to see criteria

Immediate Transfer

If "Over Time" criteria are not met, revenue is recognized when control legally transfers.

Examples: Retail store purchase, software license key delivery, signing closing papers on a spec home.

Construction: Customer's Land

Scenario

Builder constructs a house on the customer's own land.

Over Time or Point in Time?

Over Time

The customer controls the asset as it is created (it's their land).

Construction: Spec Home

Scenario

Builder builds on their own lot. Buyer signs near completion. House has alternative use.

Over Time or Point in Time?

Point in Time

Recognize at Closing.

Customer does not control asset during creation. Builder can sell to someone else.

Construction: Unique Floorplan

Scenario

Unique floorplan. Contract says if canceled, builder gets Cost + Margin. No alternative use.

Over Time or Point in Time?

Over Time

1. No alternative use (unique).

2. Enforceable right to payment.

Long-Term Contracts: Cost-to-Cost

For projects recognized "Over Time", we use Percentage-of-Completion based on costs incurred.

Raytheon Contract Data

Contract Price: $10,000,000

Est. Total Cost: $7,500,000

Year 1 Costs Incurred: $4,500,000

Calculation

% Complete = 4.5M / 7.5M = 60%

Revenue = 60% * $10M = $6,000,000

Module Summary

  • ASC 606: Revenue = Transfer of Control (Earned & Realized).
  • Cost-to-Cost: Recognize revenue progressively for long-term contracts.
  • Net AR: Gross AR minus Allowance for Doubtful Accounts.
  • DSO: Lower DSO means faster cash collection.
  • Cookie Jar Accounting: Watch out for companies 'shaving' reserves to boost Net Income.
  • Write-offs: Removing a specific bad debt does not change Net AR or Net Income.
